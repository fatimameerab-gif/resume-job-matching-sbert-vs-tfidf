# Data statement: resume-job matching study (RQ1 representation, RQ2 robustness)

Status: DRAFT for author review. Facts about the datasets were checked on the dataset pages on 3-4 Oct 2026. Numbers in section 4 and 6 were computed from `data/resume_job_pairs_v3.csv`. `[FILL]` = result still to come from the human annotation. Not legal advice.

## 1. Data policy
Only publicly available data with a stated licence or reuse terms is used. No data was collected by the authors from websites, people, or accounts, and no human participants were recruited. Two Kaggle sources carry a CC0 label declared by their uploaders although one is web scraped (Glassdoor) and the other has no documented origin (see section 2); they are handled with extra care (section 6 and 7). The one source without a licence (`mixed_domain_job_postings_157.csv`, GitHub) was removed from the pipeline and archived outside any release; the SEEK Australia dataset (CC BY 4.0) replaces it.

## 4. Constructed data
`data/resume_job_pairs_v3.csv`: resume-job pairs built by this project's code (`build_pairs_v3.py`) from datasets 1-3.
- Label = "resume category equals job category". This is **weak supervision, not human relevance judgement**; label noise is expected and not measured.
- 498 pairs = 166 unique resumes x 3 (1 positive + 2 negatives each); 25 resume categories and 25 job categories; label balance 33.3% positive / 66.7% negative. Splits are grouped by resume: train 348 / val 75 / test 75 pairs (test = 25 resumes covering 18 categories).
- Job source of the pairs: SEEK Australia 338 (67.9%), ESCO 128 (25.7%), Kaggle 672-file 32 (6.4%). The 672-file contains almost only Data Science (559) and Hadoop/data-engineer (46) titles, so it contributes few pairs.
- SEEK postings entering the pool: 563 (22 categories, at most 30 per category, fixed seed 42; categories assigned from the job TITLE by the explicit patterns in `seek_adapter.py`; Database 14, Hadoop 14, SAP 12, ETL 7, Python 6 because SEEK has few such titles). Job texts per category after de-duplication: 2 (Blockchain, Automation Testing) to 21 (Data Science); only 2 categories have <= 4 job texts (v2: 13).
- Job reuse in the main split: 12.0% of the 25 test positives use a job text that is also a training positive (the review reported 68% for v2).
- Negative tiers: random other category, same family, model-mined hard negatives. Five frozen seeds (`pairs_seed0..4`).
- A 240-pair stratified subset is annotated by humans to estimate label quality Done by two annotators: quadratic-weighted kappa 0.91, binary kappa 0.87, exact agreement 91.7%; the weak label agrees with human fit for 90.0% of the 240 pairs (anonymised scores in `RQ1_FINAL/results/human_annotation/`) (key: 80 positives + 160 negatives; SEEK 160, ESCO 62, Kaggle-672 18 items, so the weak labels of every source are audited).

## 5. Known limitations
1. **Domain mismatch.** Resumes come from a Kaggle collection; SEEK postings are Australian job ads. Category agreement is a proxy for match, so absolute scores are not comparable to real hiring outcomes.
2. **Weak labels** (see section 4); a keyword-style model can profit from category vocabulary.
3. **Category mapping.** SEEK postings were assigned to resume categories from their job TITLE by explicit patterns (`seek_adapter.py`); titles matching two categories, or none, were dropped. SEEK has no postings for 3 of the 25 resume categories (Arts, Automation Testing, Blockchain); their job texts come from ESCO only (22, 2 and 2 texts). Title-based assignment is a weak label and can be wrong for some postings; the 240-pair annotation audits it.
4. **Scraped or undocumented provenance.** SEEK and eMedCareers data were scraped by JobsPikr before release; the licence is the publisher's CC BY 4.0 release. Rights in the original SEEK advertisements cannot be verified from the JobsPikr release. The Kaggle Glassdoor postings were web scraped and the Kaggle resumes have no documented origin; their CC0 labels are the uploaders' own declarations and were not independently verified.
5. **TalentCLEF is synthetic** and has only 10 development queries, so ranking comparisons have low statistical power.
6. **Robustness lexicon** (abbreviation/expansion pairs) is curated; 131 sampled substitutions were rated by two annotators (123 judged meaning-preserving by both; raw agreement 95.4%, kappa 0.38 because of prevalence, PABAK 0.91).
7. Bias and fairness across demographic groups were not evaluated.

## 6. Personal data and ethics
- The SEEK `company_name` and `url` columns, and all other columns except title/description/category, are dropped on load and never written to outputs or releases.
- Resumes: a regex scan for e-mail, URL, LinkedIn and phone-like strings was run on the **old v2 pairs**; re-run on v3 (resume texts are lower-cased/cleaned): 166 unique resumes -> e-mail 0, URL 0, 'linkedin.com' 1, phone-like digit run 18 (may be dates/number lists); 329 unique job texts -> e-mail 0, URL 0, LinkedIn 0, phone-like 93 (mostly salary/ID numbers, not inspected). The hits were inspected by hand on 4 Oct 2026: of the 18 phone-like hits, 1 is a real phone number and the other 17 are dates or software version lists; 1 hit is a LinkedIn handle with a name; in addition 1 PAN-style id, 1 passport-style id and 1 partial e-mail address were found, and some resumes contain personal names. These identifiers were NOT removed, because every reported result was computed on this text (GPU runs cannot be repeated exactly after editing it). They come from the public source file, no identifier or name is shown in the paper, and the data is used only for this research benchmark. `scrub_pii.py` (RQ1) can remove the identifiers if a reviewer or the institution asks.
- No consent record for the resume authors is available to the project (not available). The study does not process data about identifiable individuals beyond what the public datasets contain, and no model is deployed on real candidates.
- Recruitment matching is a high-risk AI use case under the EU AI Act; the paper should state that this work is a research benchmark, not a hiring tool.
- No new data was collected from people and only public datasets were used; no ethics approval was requested. Because the origin of the resumes is undocumented, they are handled as if they could be real personal data: resume texts are not redistributed, and no resume excerpt or name appears in the paper.

## 7. Distribution
The working folder (the private copy used for the experiments) contains the texts so that every number can be reproduced. The rules below describe what may be released publicly; before any public release run `python make_public_release.py ../RQ_PUBLIC`, which removes the two raw Kaggle files and empties the resume texts and the Kaggle job texts in every pair file (tested: after putting the two Kaggle files back, `python restore_texts.py` rebuilds the pair files identical to the originals). Annotation sheets and error samples are emptied completely and can be regenerated with their scripts.

Release code, result tables, and derived pairs. For resumes and for the Kaggle Glassdoor postings only IDs, categories and labels are released, without texts; readers obtain the texts from the original Kaggle pages. SEEK and TalentCLEF texts are released with the attribution in section 3. Any shared copy of resume text is produced with `scrub_pii.py`; the results reported in the paper were computed on the unscrubbed text. Do not redistribute the raw Kaggle files, raw SEEK CSV, or ESCO files unless their terms allow it; point to the original DOIs/URLs instead. `data/_archive_unlicensed/` must never be included in any zip or repository.
